02

Web3 is forced to choose: compliance or privacy

Both sides of today's KYC model fail someone.

  • Traditional KYC hands passports, faces and home addresses to every service. One breach leaks everything.
  • Fully anonymous DeFi carries AML and sanctions risk that institutions cannot take on.
  • Screening usually happens once, at onboarding. Designations made later slip through, and revocation is manual and slow.
  • Age checks today mean collecting ID documents from children: the opposite of data minimisation.
"Trust is currently misplaced: either on a data honeypot, or on no one." GDPR and AML duties have to coexist. ZK-CID makes them complementary.
03

Prove compliance without revealing identity

And keep that status current automatically.

1A regulated issuer verifies the user off-chain (documents, sanctions screening).
2Only an anonymous Semaphore commitment is added to the on-chain compliance group.
3The user proves membership with a zero-knowledge proof made in the browser and bound to their wallet.
4ComplianceGate checks it with the real Semaphore v4 verifier, then AccessNFT mints the pass. Each proof works once.
5A Chainlink CRE workflow screens at issuance and every 5 minutes, and revokes listed members on-chain.

Chainlink CRE: the compliance lifecycle

One workflow, two triggers, one revocation path.

Handlers

0 · cronevery 5 min: re-screen every member

1 · EVM logUserAdded, finalized: screen each new credential at issuance

Pipeline

  • HTTP fetch of the sanctions feed, identical-result DON consensus
  • getMembers() / getLeaves() at the finalized block
  • Rebuild the Semaphore tree (LeanIMT + Poseidon in WASM), real Merkle siblings
  • DON-signed report → writeReport → receiver → on-tree revocation

Evidence

  • ✓Official CRE CLI 1.36 build + simulate against live Sepolia: {"status":"revoked"}
  • ✓A CRE report produced a real on-chain revocation (tx 0xb64c050e…)
  • ✓Receiver follows the official ReceiverTemplate pattern and is source-verified
  • ✓11 TestRuntime workflow tests cover both handlers; 34 contract tests

08

Regulatory fit

A technical control that maps onto existing duties. Not legal advice.

GDPR

Data minimisation (Art. 5) and privacy by design (Art. 25): only a pseudonymous commitment and a nullifier go on-chain; PII stays with the issuer.

Sanctions (OFAC, EU)

Screening at onboarding and continuously, automated revocation, and a restoration path for false positives.

Minors and age assurance

GDPR Art. 8, COPPA, UK Online Safety Act: an "over 18" group lets a service gate by age without ever storing a birth date or ID.

FATF Travel Rule, MiCA

Complementary to a VASP/CASP's own AML programme; the issuer stays accountable for KYC.

Requirement-by-requirement mapping with open gaps: zk-cid/deliverables/LEGAL_MAPPING.md

09

Who pays, and why

Proposed model: compliance that removes data liability instead of adding it.

Verifiers

DeFi pools and RWA platforms that must restrict access. Pay per verification or a subscription to gate pools without ever holding PII.

Issuers

KYC providers issue reusable ZK credentials. One check, many venues; revenue per credential without sharing customer data.

Compliance teams

Continuous screening and revocation as a managed CRE workflow, with on-chain evidence auditors can replay.

Go-to-market: start with permissioned DeFi and RWA venues that already have to KYC every investor, then add attribute groups (age, accredited investor, jurisdiction).

Why now

  • MiCA has applied in full across the EU since 30 December 2024, so crypto-asset service providers need AML controls that work on-chain.
  • UK age checks under the Online Safety Act have been enforced since 25 July 2025, which pushes services towards checking age without hoarding ID documents.
  • Sanctions designations keep changing after onboarding, and one-time screening cannot keep up.
